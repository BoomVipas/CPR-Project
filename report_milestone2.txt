CPR Project – Milestone 2 Report
================================

This milestone builds directly on the behaviours and architecture documented in `README.md`. The focus was on consolidating the carrying workflow, eliminating latent scheduler bugs, and ensuring the broker-driven teaming process succeeds even for long-range hauls. The sections below explain each change, why it was necessary, and how it integrates with the existing system.

1. Task Scheduler Robustness
----------------------------
**Problem.** Robots occasionally ran tasks out of order because we removed items from their EDF heap without re-heapifying. Python’s `heapq` requires a heap rebuild after any arbitrary removal; otherwise the very next `heappop` may surface an older task that was meant to stay buried.

**Solution.** Added a `remove_task` helper (`Simulation.step`) that deletes the specific task and immediately re-heapifies the queue. Every place that used to call `sched.h.remove(...)` now routes through this helper.

**Impact.**
- Guarantees that `sense`/`offer`/`coordinate` tasks execute at the time they were planned.
- Prevents delayed `explore` tasks from jumping ahead of urgent actions (e.g., digesting broker replies).

2. Coordinated Carry Movement
-----------------------------
**Problem.** Carry pairs approaching the deposit from different headings could thrash: we insisted the robots synchronise their facings before moving, which trapped them in rotation loops at the doorstep.

**Solution.** Reworked `coordinate_pair_movement` so both robots evaluate their candidate moves, measure distance-to-deposit and pair separation, and then:
- Accept both moves if they converge on the same tile or safely close the gap.
- Fall back to the better of the two moves if only one improves distance.
- Revert both when neither move progresses.

**Impact.** Transporting robots now glide into the deposit even when approaching from orthogonal directions; no more “waiting forever while spinning” stalemates.

3. Distance-Aware Pair TTL
--------------------------
**Problem.** The broker used a blanket `active_ttl=25` for all carrying pairs. Any gold picked far from the deposit (or through crowded corridors) would time out before arrival, causing the robots to drop their `CarryPair` state and roam aimlessly with the gold already removed from the map.

**Solution.**
- Pair brokers are now initialised with the home deposit coordinates for each team.
- When a pair becomes confirmed, the broker computes a dynamic deadline: `max(min_ttl, manhattan(cell, depot) * 2 + margin)`.
- The doubled distance plus a small buffer allows for rotations, waiting on traffic, and Lamport delays while still detecting truly stuck pairs.

**Impact.** Transport runs from the far corners of the grid complete reliably; the broker still frees up deadlocked pairs but no longer penalises legitimate long hauls.

4. Logging & Validation
-----------------------
To validate the new behaviour:

* Replayed representative scenarios (`python3 main.py --ticks 120 --seed 321 --gold 25 --print-every 120`) and watched the ASCII output to ensure deposits succeed across the board.
* Grepped simulation logs for `reason=active_timeout` after each run; the absence of hits confirms the TTL tuning works.
* Spot-checked broker event streams to verify that pair creation, confirmation, and release phases remain intact.

5. Permanent Scout Coverage
---------------------------
**Goal.** Maintain continuous reconnaissance so fresh gold sightings reach transport-capable teammates quickly.

**Work.**
- Marked the two lowest-ID robots on each team as dedicated scouts (`Simulation.__init__`). They remain in the SCOUT role, ignore help requests that would otherwise redirect them, and never keep a `CarryPair`.
- The planner gives these scouts uninterrupted `explore` cycles so they keep sweeping new territory every few ticks.

**Indicator Improvements.**
- Console output now renders scout roles as `[SCOUT*]` for easy tracking.
- The ASCII map displays permanent scouts as `S`/`s` instead of plain team glyphs, making it clear who is roaming for intel versus hauling gold.
- The detailed logger mirrors the same notation so post-run analytics can separate general scouts from the dedicated pair.

**Result.** Teams enjoy steady inflow of location updates even while transport pairs head back to base, accelerating follow-up pickups by other bots.

5. Suggested Next Steps
-----------------------
Looking ahead to future milestones:

- **Automated regression harness:** add scripted checks that fail if any active pair times out without depositing, catching future regressions quickly.
- **Dynamic congestion awareness:** consider raising the TTL margin when robots experience excessive waiting due to traffic, or adapt the route planning accordingly.
- **Unit tests for broker TTL:** isolate `PairBroker._compute_active_ttl` to verify corner cases (different grid sizes, asymmetric deposits) without running full simulations.

These updates ensure the carrying subsystem is dependable under long-range scenarios while the scouting layer keeps the team’s gold intelligence fresh, closing the gaps observed during Milestone 1 analysis and laying groundwork for more advanced coordination in Milestone 3.
